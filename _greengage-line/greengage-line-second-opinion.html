The walkable market year — a second opinion

what the first review didn't catch

The restyle is right. Turning the crater inside out from night to day was the correct call and it holds — the daylight version looks like Green Days in a way the black prototype never could, and rules 1, 3, 5 and 7 survive untouched. The 24 dates and all 8 great-turn flags match turning-days.json exactly; I diffed every row.

But the first review graded itself generously. Three of its claims don't survive contact with the build: the quarter hue does not read (ΔE 1.76 between summer and autumn), the far-wall thicket is not "partly addressed" because the 24 px cull addresses the wrong variable, and rule 4's sightline is being carried by the risers, which have no season in them at all. There are also two outright bugs it didn't mention — one of which is the loudest mark on every frame.

Fourteen findings below, ranked. I rendered the component standalone at eleven camera positions, two viewports and both light modes to check states your three figures don't show — looking backwards, February, a phone, showAxis off, laneRatio at 0.24.

Findings

01The you-are-here ring is drawn around your own feet, so it fills the screen Bug

That orange chevron arcing across the lower half of every frame is not a road marking. It is ring(a.P, 5, A.now, 2) — the 5-unit circle marking now — drawn on the ground directly beneath a camera whose eye is only 2.7 units up. At that ratio the circle projects to roughly ±250 px of screen height and the near plane clips its sides away, leaving a shallow arc that reads as a horizon line or a painted lane divider. Turning showAxis off removes it entirely (fig. 2), and the composition immediately calms down.

This is inherited from the prototype, but it got much louder in the restyle: it went from greengage at 0.95 on near-black to Spicy Paprika at 0.95 on cream — now the single highest-chroma element on screen, permanently, in every view.

FixDon't draw the ring at k=0 at all. You are standing on it; the whole design premise is that the present is the thing you cannot see. Keep the ±1 and ±2 rings — those are the ones that carry the idea — and mark now with the vertical post alone, or with nothing.
02On a turning day itself, the road and the HUD disagree Bug

The label state test is t0 >= cam.t ? 1 : 0 while the HUD's is m.t <= now. Both TODAY and every mark are pinned to 12:00, so on the actual day they are exactly equal — and the two tests fall on opposite sides. Result: the plate says Underfoot · day 1 · Mon Feb 02 while the ground paints Candlemas in Twilight Indigo, the colour reserved for what is still ahead (fig. 5). It only resolves the next day.

This misfires on precisely the 24 days a year when someone is most likely to open the thing — you'd publish a card, people would arrive on the day, and the calendar would tell them they hadn't got there yet.

FixOne shared predicate. const state = t1 <= cam.t ? -1 : (t0 > cam.t ? 1 : 0) — strict >, so equality lands on now. Then derive the HUD's here from the same function instead of re-deriving it.
03The horizon thicket is a collision problem; the 24 px cull treats it as a size problem High

if (typeSize < 24) continue removes names that are too small to read. It does nothing about names that are big enough to read and sitting on top of each other — which is the actual failure. Every daylight frame carries the same smear at the same height: Old M—Michaelmas—Marymas in fig. 1, St Vincent / Epiphany / Christmas in your own Figure 2, Lady Day / St David in fig. 5. Four to six mid-size names land within one 60 px band because that band is where the road compresses toward the vanishing point. It will never not happen.

FixScreen-space occupancy, near to far — the labels are already sorted, so it's about eight lines. Keep an array of drawn boxes; measure each candidate with ctx.measureText; skip any whose box overlaps one already drawn. Nearer wins, which is the right priority. Optionally pad the box by 0.15em so survivors breathe.
04laneRatio is doing two jobs, and the epithet is the casualty High

One number sets both how monumental the name underfoot is and how big every other name is. At 0.39 the first job works and the second doesn't. The epithet's offset is derived from the same size (p.y + size * 0.66), so on a 200 px underfoot name first bread lands about a third of a screen below Lammas, on a different paving slab, reading as an unrelated caption (fig. 1). Meanwhile the glut sits snug under St Bartholomew because that name is small.

Drop laneRatio to 0.24 and everything composes (fig. 6) — thicket gone, epithet attached — but now you can read Lammas comfortably, which breaks load-bearing rule 2. That's the tell: the number is masking two separate bugs rather than being the lever.

FixSplit it. A nearScale for the underfoot and next-ahead name (keep 0.39, or push higher), a midScale around 0.24 for everything beyond. And decouple the epithet: cap its offset at min(size*0.66, 34px) and its size at min(size*0.36, 30px), so it stays a whisper under the name at every distance instead of scaling into a second headline.
05The quarter hue does not read — the numbers say so High

The review claims the quarter hue now actually reads. It doesn't. The four paving hues are 208 / 152 / 44 / 26 at S 17%, L 86–90%. Measured as ΔE76 between the flat fills:

PairΔE76Reads?
Summer (44) vs Autumn (26)1.76No — below the just-noticeable threshold
Spring (152) vs Summer (44)4.07Not across a fog gradient at a glancing angle
Winter (208) vs Spring (152)4.55Same
Winter (208) vs Summer (44)5.95Best case, still marginal
Spent ground vs any quarter9.8–11.7Yes — spent/ahead is the only distinction landing

Compare fig. 1 (August, custard bucket) with fig. 5 (February, blue bucket): the road is the same cream in both. What actually separates the two frames is the sky and the walls, not the paving. Load-bearing rule 4 — far terraces separating by hue — is not being met; the risers are doing that work, and they are a fixed blue-grey regardless of season.

Separately, the buckets are wrong. Math.floor(f*4) on year-fraction puts the boundaries at 1 Jan / 1 Apr / 1 Jul / 1 Oct. Green Days' own SEASON_MONTHS uses Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec. The paving's seasons are two months out of step with the app's.

FixMove the season onto the riser, not the tread. The riser is the surface you see edge-on across the crater — it is where hue separation would actually do the job rule 4 asks for — and it can carry real chroma without turning the road you're standing on into a colour chart. Then fix the boundaries to the app's quarters, and if the tread keeps any tint at all, carry it in lightness (86 → 91) as well as hue so it survives the fog.
06“Across the crater” prints a name and a date that don't belong to each other High

The panel reads Candlemas · Sat Feb 06 2027 · half a turn. Candlemas is 2 February. The date shown is today + 182.6 days; the name shown is the last turning day at or before that date. Two different facts stacked with a middot between them, which any reader will parse as "Candlemas, 6 February." For most of the year the gap runs up to three weeks.

The left-hand plate gets this exactly right — day 7 · Fri Aug 07 keeps the offset visible. The right-hand plate drops it. On a publication whose entire proposition is a calendar you can trust, this is the one class of error you cannot ship.

FixSay the same thing the left plate says: Candlemas · day 5 · Sat Feb 06 2027. Or drop the date and name the day only. Either is honest; the current pairing isn't.
07Paprika now has two roles, which is the one thing Template Law forbids it Medium

Your own grounding note says Spicy Paprika — epithets and the now-marker, one role only. That sentence contains two roles. And the steal you pulled off the 1908 Osaka sheet was specifically red as class, not emphasis — one category of thing, across all 24 cards, forever.

Right now paprika means "this is the working name" and "this is the present instant," and the second use is the loudest mark on screen (see finding 01). Whichever survives, the other has to go somewhere else.

FixEpithets keep paprika — that's the card system's established role and the walk should inherit it. Give the present marker Seagrass, or Metallic Gold if it needs to sit apart from the ground. It's a one-line change in both theme blocks.
08Mobile is broken in two ways, one of them structural High

At 390 px the two top plates overlap outright (fig. 3) — the left one is capped at 44vw and wraps to four lines, the right one isn't capped at all, and they collide across the middle of the screen. The bottom-left instruction pill runs under the bottom-right control plate. Four fixed-corner plates is a desktop pattern.

The structural half is worse: there is no way to step on a phone. Walking is bound to wheel and ArrowUp/Down, neither of which exists on touch, and pointermove is already claimed by look. A phone visitor can look around and toggle auto-walk. They cannot move to a chosen day — which is the entire interaction.

FixTwo plates on small screens, not four: merge the masthead and card numeral into one top bar, collapse the instruction line into the control plate. For walking, add a vertical drag zone or a two-finger drag, or simplest — a slim scrubbable strip of the 24 numerals along one edge, which doubles as the year index and would be useful on desktop too.

The epithet is the worst case and the most exposed: it's set in a thin handwriting face at 0.36× the name, in the lowest-contrast colour on the page. At distance it dissolves into noise — visible as the pink fuzz in the horizon smear in fig. 1.

FixDarken the epithet on the road only — #c9410f gets you to 4.1:1 on cream and still reads as paprika alongside the cards. The gold year wants the same treatment (#8a6a1e). 10The eight great turns are encoded in a weight nobody can compare Medium

great maps to Nunito 800 instead of 600. Weight is a comparative signal — it needs two samples at the same size in the same eyeful. On this road every name is at a different size and a different distance, so the distinction is invisible in every one of the eleven frames I rendered. The card system's whole spine is that eight of the 24 carry the year and sixteen fill between them; the walk currently doesn't know that.

FixMake it structural, not typographic. A full-width inlay band across the tread at a great turn — a threshold you physically cross — or a taller riser, or the Roman numeral cut into the paving. Any of those survives the distance; 200 units of font weight doesn't.
11The density lever you recommended is a four-step staircase — use slot length instead Medium

The open-questions box proposes wiring slab texture to each day's in-season count. I pulled the field: in_season_at_midpoint.items takes only about eight distinct values across the 24 days, in repeating blocks — 33, 33, 21, 21, 24, 24, 24, 24, 64, 64, 69, 69, 69, 69, 73… It is downstream of produce.json's four-season quantisation, so wiring it would paint four flat bands and read as a discovery when it is really the hardcoded season constant coming back out. That's the same tautology the data notes already flag.

days is the field you want. It runs 7 to 25, it varies day by day, and it is editorial — Santiago holds 7 days, Martinmas holds 25. That is the real texture of the market year, and it is the one number that would make January feel empty and August feel crowded without claiming a measurement you don't have.

FixScale SUBDIV and slab length by days, so short slots are a few quick paces and long ones are a long walk. Then add the 1908 大/小 long-short mark to the HUD — one glyph, and the card system's borrowed device finally does something.
12The 24 days are a hardcoded copy of turning-days.json Note

All 24 opening dates and all 8 great-turn flags match canon exactly — I diffed them. But they're pasted into the component, not read from the file, so the next edit to turning-days.json silently desynchronises the walk. Given how much of this project's credibility rests on one source of truth, that's worth closing before it bites.

The epithets are lowercase truncations of the canonical working_name, and in 14 of 24 cases the half that got cut is the half with the produce in it: Bartholomew wipes the rain / the glut → the glut; St Luke's little summer / the last tomatoes loses the tomatoes; Michaelmas geese / the squashes loses the squashes. On a walk called the market year, the road is showing the saint half and dropping the market half.

The three double names also resolve inconsistently — Sant Jordi over St George, but Midsummer over São João and Martinmas over São Martinho. For an anglophone-traveller audience in southern Europe, pick one rule and hold it.

FixImport the JSON. Then decide the epithet rule deliberately: either the produce half everywhere, or alternate by state — saint-lore for what's ahead, produce for what's underfoot.
13Nothing here is readable by anything that isn't a browser with a mouse High

A bare <canvas>, no aria-label, no fallback content, no text in the DOM except the HUD chrome. Screen readers get nothing. Search engines get nothing. Link previews get nothing.

That's not only an accessibility point. The known problem on greendays.day is that only one page of twenty-three is indexed and the content layer is orphaned. Shipping another canvas-only page adds a twenty-fourth orphan. Meanwhile the thing you'd need to fix it is already in the file: 24 named days, each with a date, a Roman numeral, a working name and a slot length — which is exactly the shape of page that indexes well and gets linked.

FixPut the 24 days in the DOM as a real list behind the canvas — visually hidden if you like, or better, visible below the fold as the static index. It becomes the fallback, the screen-reader content, the crawlable page and the no-JS view in one move. Add a title, description and an OG image while you're there.
14Cheap engine wins that are worth taking now Note

The occlusion test is O(labels × quads). Around 100 candidate labels against roughly 2,000 quads, point-in-polygon, every frame — that's a few hundred thousand tests per frame before anything is drawn. A screen-space bounding-box reject in front of inPoly removes almost all of it.

The rAF loop never idles. It redraws forever even when nothing has changed. On a phone that's a warm battery for a page you'd want people to leave open.

ctx.letterSpacing is Chromium-only. The tracking-tightens-as-it-approaches effect — trackFar 0.30, one of the nicer ideas here — silently does nothing in Firefox and older Safari. It degrades gracefully, so it isn't urgent, but don't count it as shipped.

FixBounding-box reject before inPoly; run the loop only while walking or a pointer is down, otherwise draw on demand (the wheel handler already does this). For tracking, either accept the Chromium-only bonus or draw the distant names glyph-by-glyph, which you'd want anyway for curving type to the road.

What I'd do, in order

  1. An hour. Drop the k=0 ring (01). Make the state predicate strict and share it with the HUD (02). Fix the "across the crater" readout to name a day and its own day-number (06). Give the present marker a colour that isn't paprika (07).
  2. An afternoon. Screen-space collision culling for labels (03). Split laneRatio into near and mid, and cap the epithet's offset and size (04). Darken the epithet and the year numeral (09). Bounding-box reject in the occlusion loop, and idle the rAF (14).
  3. A day. Move the season hue to the risers and fix the quarter boundaries to SEASON_MONTHS (05). Import turning-days.json instead of the paste, and settle the epithet rule (12). Put the 24 days in the DOM as the fallback and the indexable page (13).
  4. The next design pass. Give the eight great turns a structural mark (10). Scale slab length by days and add the long-short glyph (11). Rebuild the HUD as two plates that work at 390 px, and find the touch gesture that walks (08).

Where I might be wrong

  • Finding 05 is a measurement of flat fills. Under fog, at a glancing angle, across a whole field, hue difference can carry further than ΔE suggests — but 1.76 between summer and autumn isn't rescued by any of that, and the side-by-side of figures 1 and 5 is the honest test.
  • Finding 10 assumes the eight great turns should be legible in the walk. If the walk is meant to be the undifferentiated year and the cards carry the hierarchy, then weight-only is a defensible restraint. I don't think that's what you want, but it's a real position.
  • Finding 04's split adds a second magic number where you had one. That's a genuine cost. The alternative — keep 0.39 and rely on collision culling alone — would fix the thicket but leave the epithet stranded.
