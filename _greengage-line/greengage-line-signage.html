Rule 2 gets better, not worse

You cannot read your own town's sign once you're in the town. The old rule said the moment underfoot is too big to read — but that was a font-size trick, and at laneRatio 0.24 it stopped being true. Here it's a fact about where the sign is: you passed it. Turn round and it's behind you, struck through.

The cost is honest: this repeals rule 7. Type stops belonging to the surface. I think that's right — rule 7 was written for a black void where a floating label had nothing to stand on. In a daylit crater an upright object on a post is more physical, not less. But call it a repeal, not an extension.

What I'd still watch

  • One grammar, held. The Swiss fingerpost is the right answer to across the crater — stacked arms, sorted, at fixed size — but take the geometry and leave the yellow. Two plate species maximum: the Ortstafel for turning days, the fingerpost for the sightline.
  • Does it still feel like a place? This is the real risk and these two frames don't settle it. The road is emptier and more diagrammatic than the painted version. Walking it is the only test.
  • Plates on other terraces need a year. Figure B has two Cold Sophias on screen with nothing distinguishing them.
  • The posts are doing very little. They read as thin grey ticks against the wall. Either commit to real posts with a base and a shadow on the paving, or drop them and let the plates float — which would be a different, more abstract proposition.
  • Ship findings 01, 02, 06 and 07 regardless. They're bugs in the current build and they'd be bugs in this one.
