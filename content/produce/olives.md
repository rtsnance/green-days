*Azeitona* in Portugal, *aceituna* in Spain — and neither comes from Latin *oliva* at all. Both are Arabic, *az-zaytuna*, from *zayt*, oil; Portuguese still calls olive oil *azeite* and every other oil *óleo*. France, Italy and Germany kept the Latin word. Nobody eats one off the tree: raw fruit is loaded with oleuropein, bitter rather than dangerous, and only brine, lye or long salting takes it out. Green and black are the same fruit at different ages — the table ones come in green from September, the oil ones hang until November. Simplest thing to do with it: warm them with orange peel and a bay leaf.
