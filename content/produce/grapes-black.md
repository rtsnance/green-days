*Uvas pretas* in Portugal, *uva nera* in Italy, *raisin noir* in France — but *blaue Trauben* in Germany and *blauwe druiven* in the Netherlands, so one fruit is black, blue or red depending where you stand. A currant is this same fruit again: the word is worn down from *raisins de Corinthe*, the tiny dried grapes shipped out of Corinth, and the bush berries only borrowed it in the 1570s, on a resemblance. An old recipe calling for currants may mean dried grapes. Buy them dark and firm on green stems; wrinkled or dropping means old. Simplest thing to do with it: roast the bunch whole and spoon it over cheese.
