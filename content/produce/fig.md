*Figo* in Portugal, *figue* in France, *fico* in Italy, *higo* in Spain — but the more useful word is *lampo*. A fig tree crops twice: the *figo lampo*, which the French call the *figue-fleur*, ripens in June on last year's wood, and this, the main crop, ripens now on this year's. Watch for one more Portuguese trap — a *figo-da-índia* is not a fig but a prickly pear, off a cactus. Buy them soft, heavy and slightly split at the base; a firm fig has stopped sweetening and will never start again. Simplest thing to do with it: tear it open and eat it with something salty.
