*Swede* is short for Swedish turnip, and the word breaks the moment you travel. Scotland, Ireland, Cornwall and northern England call this one the turnip, leaving the small white root to take a qualifier; America says *rutabaga*, from a Swedish dialect word whose second half the sources gloss as either bag or lump. Portugal barely grows it, except in Trás-os-Montes, where it is *rabas*. The Halloween lantern was carved from a turnip long before the pumpkin — by whichever meaning of the word. Frost genuinely improves it, so the late ones are the sweet ones. Simplest thing to do with it: mash it hard with butter and too much pepper.
