*Uvas brancas* in Portugal, *raisin blanc* in France, *uva bianca* in Italy — white, not green; only the Danes and Swedes see green ones. The French word is the trap: a *raisin* is the fresh grape and the dried one is a *raisin sec*, so English took the word and handed it to the wrong fruit. Its root is the harvest — *vindima*, *vendimia*, *vendange* — and the Revolution's Vendémiaire opened on a day named simply Raisin. Grapes never ripen once cut, so ignore the fruit and read the stem: green and pliable, never brown. Simplest thing to do with it: freeze a bunch and eat them like sweets.
