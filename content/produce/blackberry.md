*Amora* in Portugal, *mora* in Spain and Italy, *mûre* in France, *Brombeere* in Germany — and the Iberian word is older than the fruit it usually names. *Amora* comes from the Latin *morum*, the mulberry, and took in the bramble only by analogy, so a stall selling *amoras* may mean either; the tree is the *amoreira*. Telling the fruit from a raspberry is easier than telling the words apart: the pale core stays inside a picked blackberry, while a raspberry comes away hollow. English lore says pick them before Michaelmas, which is really frost, flies and mould. Simplest thing to do with it: eat them warm, standing at the bramble.
