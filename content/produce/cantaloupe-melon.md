*Meloa* in Portugal — and it is not a small *melão*, it is a different melon. *Melão* is the big pale one; *meloa* is this, round and ribbed and orange inside, sold by the armful through August. *Melancia*, one letter further along, is the watermelon. The name is said to come from Cantalupo, a papal estate outside Rome where the seed was grown in the eighteenth century, and in North America it has drifted onto a heavily netted muskmelon instead. Sniff the blossom end: sweet and musky means ready, nothing at all means a week early. Simplest thing to do with it: quarter it cold and lay cured ham over it.
