*Ameixa* in Portugal, *ciruela* in Spain, *susina* in Italy, *Pflaume* in Germany — and *prune* in France, which is the one that catches English speakers out, because a French *prune* is the fresh fruit and the dried one they are reaching for is a *pruneau*. Ask for prunes in Agen and you will be handed plums. Buy by feel rather than by colour: the dusty bloom on the skin is the fruit's own wax, not dirt, and a sign it has not been over-handled. Plump with a slight give, never rock-hard. Simplest thing to do with it: halve, stone, and roast cut-side up until the edges blister.
